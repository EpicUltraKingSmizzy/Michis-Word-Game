# Michi's Word Game design questions

These are the non-coding questions that came up while building features for Michi's Word Game. Each one has the answer I picked and a one-line reason. Change any answer you like and the code will follow.

The dictionary files are listed in `WordListPart` (lib/logic/word_list.dart), and the words removed from the core list are in `tool/dict/removed_words.txt`. The numbers for the missed words live in `MissedWordsTuning` (lib/logic/missed_words.dart). The replay and word list are the `missed` stage in lib/ui/screens/results_screen.dart, and its grey-silver colours are `MwgColors.silver`, `silverEdge` and `silverLine` (lib/ui/theme.dart).

## A. End-of-game missed words

1. **Which games show the missed words?** Only a completed solo round: you opened a room and started the game with nobody else in it. Multiplayer rooms and challenge links don't show it. *Why:* you asked for it after a Solo Game, and in a group it would mean showing words nobody found, which is a different feature.
2. **What about a round that was quit early?** It never shows. Quitting goes straight home and skips the results screen entirely, so only a round that ran to the buzzer gets there. *Why:* it's a reward for finishing, and a quit round isn't scored.
3. **Where in the end-of-game flow does it appear?** Right after the word-by-word scoring and the final score (after you tap past it), and before the highlights, the longest word and the winner pages. *Why:* that's where you said it should go, after the found words and the points.
4. **How are the missed words shown?** *(Changed, your decision.)* On the board itself, traced letter by letter with exactly the same path animation and timing as the words you found during scoring. As each word is traced it also joins a list between Michi's bubble and the board (see section D). *Why:* you asked for it, and seeing where the word was hiding on the grid is more useful than a plain list.
5. **What colour are the missed words?** *(Changed, your decision.)* Grey-silver: each tile turns silver with dark letters as the trace swipes over it, joined by a light silver line, and the word pill above the board is silver too. A missed word stays silver when its trace finishes, instead of turning green like a found word. *Why:* you picked grey-silver, and green would make it look like you'd scored it.
6. **What does Michi say?** *(Changed, your decision.)* Michi pops up from the bottom with a speech bubble: "Here are 10 words you missed!", using the real count when there are fewer ("Here are 4 words you missed!", or "Here is 1 word you missed!"). *Why:* your wording, kept accurate when fewer than 10 are left.
7. **Are any points shown for missed words?** *(Changed, your decision.)* No. There's no point value next to the words, and the scores don't change. *Why:* you said there's no need for it, and showing points you didn't get could be confusing.
8. **What happens if I press Skip during scoring?** Skip still only skips the scoring animation. After the final scores, the missed-words replay starts right away and plays in full. *Why:* that matches how Skip already worked: it skips scoring, and the pages after it still show.
9. **Can I skip the missed-words replay?** *(Changed, follows from your decision.)* Yes. A Skip button shows while the missed words are being traced. Pressing it ends the replay and puts every word in the list straight away, ready to tap. It no longer jumps to the highlights; you move on with the Continue button. *Why:* now that you can tap the words, skipping straight past them would throw that away.
10. **What counts as a missed word?** Any word the game's solver finds on the board (using the round's minimum word length) that isn't in your list of found words. *Why:* that's the same word list the scoring and the "words hiding in this grid" count use, so it can't disagree with them.
11. **How many words are shown?** Up to 10 (`MissedWordsTuning.count`). *Why:* the number you asked for, and the replay stays short (about 10 seconds).
12. **Which lengths come first?** Words of 3 to 5 letters (`MissedWordsTuning.shortMin` and `shortMax`). If the round's minimum length is 4 or 5, the shorter ones simply don't exist on the board. *Why:* these are the everyday words a player is most likely to kick themselves over.
13. **How are the 10 picked when there are more than 10 short missed words?** At random, but the random pick is seeded by the board's letters, so the same board always shows the same list. *Why:* random keeps it from always showing words starting with A, and the seed keeps the list from changing if it's rebuilt.
14. **What if there are fewer than 10 short missed words?** The rest are filled with longer missed words, shortest first (random within the same length). *Why:* the next-easiest words are the most useful ones to see.
15. **What if I found every 3-5 letter word?** The replay shows longer words you missed instead, and Michi says "You found every short word! Here are 4 longer words you missed!" (with the real count). *Why:* that's what you asked for, and it fits Michi's new line while giving you credit for clearing the short words.
16. **How is the longest word kept out?** Every word that ties for the longest length on the board is left out, which is exactly the list the longest-word page reveals from (`pickLongestWord` / `allLongest`). *Why:* the longest word has its own reveal, and with a tie the reveal can show any of them, so none of them are spoiled early.
17. **What if fewer than 10 words are left after all that?** It replays however many there are, and Michi's line uses that count. *Why:* a short honest list is better than padding it with the longest word.
18. **What if there are no missed words at all?** The replay is skipped and you go straight to the highlights. *Why:* an empty board with Michi talking about 0 words would be a pointless extra tap.
19. **What order are the words traced in?** By length, shortest first, then alphabetical. *Why:* it builds up to the longer words, like a small countdown to the longest-word reveal.
20. **Does the board stay in the same place as during scoring?** Yes on most phones. The replay uses the same layout, with Michi's bubble and the word list where the score banners were, so the grid doesn't jump between the two replays. On short screens (less than about 600 points of room) the board moves down a little to give the list more space. *Why:* it reads as one continuous replay, and on small phones fitting the words matters more.
21. **Are odd dictionary words (place names and so on) filtered out here?** No, it uses the dictionary as it is. *Why:* the dictionary is being cleaned up separately, and once that lands this replay gets the benefit automatically.

## B. Dictionary: slang, profanity and obscure words

22. **Where do the new slang words go?** *(Your decision.)* Into their own file, `assets/dict/slang.txt.gz` (2,000 words). The game loads it along with the main list `words.txt.gz`. *Why:* you want the game to use them, but you might add a way to turn them off for some features later.
23. **Where do the new profanity words go?** *(Your decision.)* Into their own file too, `assets/dict/profanity.txt.gz` (110 words). It's also loaded by default. *Why:* same reason. It keeps the option to switch them off.
24. **How would a feature turn a list off later?** Pass `WordListOptions(slang: false)` or `WordListOptions(profanity: false)` when building the dictionary. The default `WordListOptions.all` loads everything, and `WordListOptions.coreOnly` loads just the main list. Nothing uses the switches yet. *Why:* a simple on/off per file is all a future toggle needs, and the game behaves the same until then.
25. **Is the profanity already in the main list split out too?** No. Only the 110 new profanity words are in the separate file. The main list keeps its own profanity, as before. *Why:* you said there's no need to sort the existing dictionary for that now.
26. **Does the slur blocklist still work?** Yes. `blocklist.txt` is applied to every list (main, slang and profanity) when the game loads. *Why:* a slur shouldn't sneak back in through one of the new files.
27. **What counts as "slang" for the additions?** Any word a typical English speaker would recognise in casual use today that the game used to reject. That includes true slang (bruh, yeet, sus, rizz, droid, roid), informal and internet words (lol, noob, hangry, adorbs) and everyday modern words that were missing (app, cafe, donut, lockdown, offline, gameplay). *Why:* "app" or "donut" being rejected is just as annoying as "bruh", and there aren't 2,000 pure slang words common enough to qualify.
28. **How common does a slang word have to be?** A base word needs a frequency of at least 2.0 on the wordfreq Zipf scale (about once per 10 million words; "droid" is 3.2). A plural or verb form needs at least 1.3, and only if its base word is in. Every word also needs a real lowercase entry in Wiktionary, and I read through all of them by hand to take out names, brands, abbreviations and jargon. *Why:* frequency measures "common", and the dictionary check plus a human read keeps names like "Kanye" out.
29. **Are plurals and verb forms of slang words included?** Yes, when they're in real use: droid and droids, roid and roids, collab, collabs, collabed and collabing. *Why:* finding "droid" but having "droids" rejected feels broken.
30. **Are letter-by-letter abbreviations (idk, tbh, wtf, omg), dropped-g spellings (talkin) or joined contractions (dont, ive) included?** No. Only abbreviations people say as a word are in (lol, yolo, fomo). *Why:* they aren't words, and things like "dont" and "ill" would confuse more than help.
31. **What about slurs and hate terms in the slang?** Left out completely. They're not in either file (for example tranny, libtard, crip, chav, thot, cuck). *Why:* slurs never belong in the game.
32. **Which words were removed from the main list?** The 5,000 obscure words you approved: proper nouns that aren't well known (places, people, peoples, religions, brands), archaic, dialect and obsolete words, words with no English dictionary entry, old variant spellings, and rare technical words. *Why:* players have never heard of them, or know they aren't allowed in word games.
33. **What happened to the plurals and verb forms of removed words?** 286 more were removed, but only when they're clearly the same obscure word: a regular plural or verb form (‑s, ‑es, ‑ed, ‑ing, ‑er, ‑est, ‑ies) that my obscure-word filter had also flagged on its own, and not a proper noun when the base wasn't one (or the reverse). Examples: bedewed and bedews (from bedew), slavs (from slav), wambling (from wamble). Look-alikes that are really different words were kept, like atoned (not from "aton"), glomming (glom, not "glomma"), jesus, elias, luging and inlier. All removed words are listed in `tool/dict/removed_words.txt`. *Why:* you shouldn't be able to play "bedews" when "bedew" is gone, but a coincidental spelling match mustn't take out a real word.
34. **Which words are never removed?** Anything in the mainstream 12dicts list (2of12inf), anything in SCOWL size 35 or below, and any common non-proper word (Zipf 3.0 or higher). A few hand-picked words are also kept: nerf, fides, doric. *Why:* if a mainstream dictionary list has it, or people use it often, it stays.
35. **How was the 5,000 chosen?** By a score that adds up how rare the word is, what kind of word it is (proper nouns and archaic words count most), how rare it is in SCOWL, and a bonus for short words (3 to 7 letters). *Why:* short odd words like "fley" or "ersh" show up on boards and in the missed-words replay all the time, while very long ones rarely fit on a board.
36. **Some places and names you want were in that list (for example a few capital cities). What happens to them?** They're removed for now, as approved. Places and given names that meet your new criteria go on a separate list of words to add, for you to approve next. *Why:* the 5,000 list was approved as it is, and putting places and names back is its own change.
37. **Do old challenge links still work?** Yes. A link made with the old dictionary still opens and shows the scores. If its word list doesn't match the new dictionary, it leaves out the words (that's how challenge links already handle a different dictionary version). *Why:* it's the existing safety net for dictionary changes.
38. **What if two players have different versions (an old APK and the new web build)?** The host's dictionary decides. Your phone accepts a word if its own dictionary has it, but the host only scores words its dictionary also has. So with an old-version host, a new slang word like "droid" won't score, and a removed word can still score with an old host. *Why:* that's how the game already handles any dictionary difference, and it fixes itself once everyone updates (the next APK will include the new lists).

## C. Dictionary: places, people's names, duplicates and the second round of removals

39. **Which places and names are in the game now?** Everything in the reviewed lists. That's 628 new place words (cities of 1 million or more, capitals, famous cities, provinces of 2 million or more, countries and their other names, seas, rivers, landmarks), 3,396 new given names and nicknames (the top ~2,000 male and ~2,000 female names in the world plus nicknames), and all the places and names that were already in the dictionary. *(Changed, your decision.)* This replaces entry 36: places from the first 5,000 removals that meet the new rules, such as riga, reims, kiel and banjul, are back. *Why:* you approved the places, names and kept lists.
40. **Where do they go?** In two new files, `assets/dict/places.txt.gz` and `assets/dict/person_names.txt.gz`. Both load by default, and `WordListOptions(places: false)` or `WordListOptions(personNames: false)` leaves one out for a future toggle. The slur blocklist still applies to every file. *Why:* it's the same setup as slang and profanity, so a feature can turn them off later.
41. **Which existing words moved into those files?** Only words that are nothing but a place name or a person's name: korea, paris, london, georgia, robert, michael, kubrick. A word with any other use stays in the main list: rob, bill, mark, will, jack, grace, bob, turkey, guinea, china, nice, reading. *Why:* this is your rule. Switching names or places off later must never take away an ordinary word.
42. **How did you decide whether a word has another use?** A word stays in the main list if any of these is true:
    - Wiktionary has a lowercase entry for it that isn't just a name, even an old one (jordan, a type of chamber pot).
    - The capitalised form is also used as an adjective, noun or verb (Persian, Latino, Sunday, a David-and-Goliath "David").
    - It's in the 12dicts list or the Hunspell dictionaries in lowercase.
    - It's a plural or spelling of another word the game already accepts (palau as a spelling of pilaf).

    *Why:* moving a word that's also a normal word would be the worse mistake, so when in doubt it stays in the main list.
43. **How do you tell a place from a person?** The approved place list comes first. Otherwise I use the first meaning in Wiktionary: Georgia (the country) is a place, Michael is a name and Belisarius (a general) is a name. Proper nouns that are neither, such as months, stars (sirius), holidays (pentecost) and books of the Bible (isaiah), stay in the main list. *Why:* the main meaning is what players will think of.
44. **What about words that are only surnames (kubrick, asimov, austen)?** They go in person_names too. There are about 775 of them. *Why:* they're only names, so they fit your "names that are just names" rule. Keeping them all in one file means one toggle covers every person's name.
45. **What happened to place additions that also have another meaning?** 64 of the 628 went into the main list instead of places, because they're also ordinary words or demonyms. Examples: agra (Irish for "darling"), sark (a shirt), kara (a Sikh bangle), porto (port wine), naija (Nigerian), ural. *Why:* this is the same rule as entry 41. Whatever file they're in, they're all playable.
46. **The 13 words that are both a new place and a new name (lara, kara, ravi, mariana...)?** They're only in places (or the main list if they have another meaning). *(Your decision.)* *Why:* a word lives in exactly one file.
47. **How are duplicates prevented?** `tool/dict/check_duplicates.py` fails if a word appears twice in one file or in more than one file. The same check is a test in `test/dictionary_test.dart`, which the web deploy runs (`flutter test`) before building. When a word fits more than one list, it goes in the most specific one: profanity, then slang, then places, then person_names, then the main list. *Why:* turning a list off should remove exactly that list's words, and the order puts each word under the toggle it most belongs to. For example, yas and yolo stay in slang rather than moving to places or names.
48. **Which flagged words were removed?** Exactly the 20,818 you kept in your edited flagged_removals file. None of them were on the kept or name lists, so nothing was skipped. They're added to `tool/dict/removed_words.txt`. *Why:* you chose them yourself.
49. **What about the plural or verb forms of those words?** No extras were removed this time. 198 forms of removed words (for example bootlickers, allegorized) were on the flagged list and you took them off, so they stay. About 100 more were never flagged and are real words in their own right (excruciating, dilapidated, heroically). *Why:* in Part 1, I added inflections the filter had flagged. This time you saw every flagged form and chose, so your list is final.
50. **Is any blocklisted word in the new files?** No. A blocked word that only looks like a name stays in the main list, where the blocklist removes it when the game loads. *Why:* that keeps the place and name files free of slurs.
51. **Will rebuilding the main list bring moved or removed words back?** No. `tool/build_dictionary.py` leaves out every word in removed_words.txt and every word already in another list file. *Why:* rebuilding should never undo these decisions.

## D. Missed words list and tapping a word

52. **Where do the missed words appear as they're revealed?** *(Changed, your decision.)* In a centered, wrapping row of silver pills under Michi's speech bubble and above the board. Each word pops into the list the moment its trace starts, so the list grows one word at a time. *Why:* your design. It lets you read all the words at once while still watching each one on the grid.
53. **Is the single word pill that used to sit above the board still there?** No, the list replaces it. The word being traced is the one that stands out in the list (white outline, slightly bigger). *Why:* showing the same word twice would waste the space the list needs.
54. **Are there points on the pills?** No, just the word. *Why:* you asked for no points on missed words earlier, and that still holds.
55. **When can I tap a word?** *(Changed, your decision.)* Once all the words have played (or after Skip). Michi's line stays the same, and a small hint under the list says "Tap a word to see it on the board". *Why:* you asked for it after the animation finishes, and tapping mid-replay would fight with the replay.
56. **What does tapping a word do?** It traces that word on the board again with the same silver letter-by-letter animation, then leaves its path lit in silver. Its pill stands out in the list. Tapping another word switches to that one, and tapping the same word again replays it. *Why:* the moving trace shows the order of the letters, which a static highlight can't.
57. **How do I move on to the highlights now?** With a Continue button at the bottom of the screen, instead of tapping anywhere. Tapping the board or empty space on this page does nothing. *Why:* taps now pick words, so a stray tap shouldn't skip you past them. The other results pages still use tap-anywhere.
58. **What if 10 long words don't fit on a small phone?** On short screens the pills use smaller text and tighter spacing, and the list scrolls if it still doesn't fit. Ten words fit without scrolling on a 320 x 568 screen (iPhone SE size). *Why:* the board and the Continue button always need to stay on screen.

## E. Dictionary: surnames

59. **Are surnames in the game?** Yes. *(Changed, your decision.)* The 1,000 most popular surnames in the world that weren't already in the dictionary (martinez, sharma, nguyen, kaur, ivanov...) are in a new file, `assets/dict/surnames.txt.gz`. *Why:* you approved the list and asked for its own file.
60. **Can surnames be switched off?** Yes. They load by default, like every other list, and `WordListOptions(surnames: false)` leaves them out. The blocklist applies to them too. *Why:* it's the same setup as slang, profanity, places and person_names.
61. **What happened to the surnames that were in person_names?** 658 words that are only surnames (kubrick, asimov, beethoven, babbage) moved from person_names to surnames. *(Changed, your decision.)* This replaces entry 44, which had put them in person_names. *Why:* you want surnames in their own file.
62. **What about a word that is both a surname and a given name (byron, calvin, carson, anderson)?** It stays in person_names. That's 117 words. It counts as a given name if it's in the top world given names or their nicknames, in the US first-name list, or if Wiktionary lists it as a given name. *Why:* most players will think of the first name, and a word can only be in one file.
63. **Where do surnames fit in the duplicate order?** Just after person_names: profanity, then slang, then places, then person_names, then surnames, then the main list. The duplicate checker and its test now include surnames.txt.gz. *Why:* this matches entry 62. A word that is also a given name belongs under the given-names toggle. A word that is only a surname still beats the main list, because if it had another meaning it would have stayed in the main list.
64. **What are wei, sonu and gnu?** "wei" is mostly a Chinese surname (Wei, as in the Wei dynasty), so it moved to surnames. *(Your decision.)* "sonu" is a common Indian given name and nickname, so it stays in person_names. "gnu" is a real word (the wildebeest), so it stays in the main list. *Why:* names go in the matching names file, and real words stay in the main list.
65. **Some approved surnames were in the first 5,000 removals (rios, lugo, baez, braga, gond, jena, maurya, soong, dou). Which decision wins?** The surnames list. Those 9 are back, and they're no longer in `tool/dict/removed_words.txt`. *(Changed, your decision.)* *Why:* the first removals were approved as "obscure proper nouns". Approving these words as popular surnames is the newer, more specific choice. It works the same way as the places that came back in entry 39. None of the 20,818 words you picked yourself came back.

## F. Rejected words and 15 more three-letter words

66. **Which three-letter words were added to the dictionary?** The 15 you picked: vue, fel, zha, dvd, wtf, com, cia, fbi, dna, lbs, cum, rom, eco, doo, ufo. *(Your decision.)* *Why:* they have real meanings people will try to play.
67. **Which file did each go in?**
    - slang: dvd, cia, fbi, dna, ufo, lbs, com, eco, rom, doo, vue
    - profanity: wtf, cum
    - person_names: fel
    - surnames: zha

    *Why:* the acronyms and modern words are everyday words that aren't in the core SCOWL list, which is what the slang file is for (like app, cafe and donut). wtf stands for a swear word, and cum is mostly used in its sexual sense, so both go where the profanity toggle covers them. fel is mainly a given name or nickname (Felix), and zha is mainly a Chinese surname. vue is a name, but it's also the Vue framework and "vue" as in "point of view", so it isn't only a name and goes in slang. The duplicate check still passes.
68. **What is the rejected words list?** `assets/dict/rejected_words.txt.gz` holds 16,415 combinations: every three-letter combination from aaa to zzz that isn't in a dictionary file. It's the review's rejected list plus everything in the "excluded acronyms" and "unsure" files, minus the 15 words above. *(Changed, your decision.)* The review had kept those acronyms and unsure words off the list, but you chose to reject them. Every three-letter combination is now either a dictionary word or rejected. *Why:* this is the starting list for the failed-words feature.
69. **Why is it an app asset rather than a tool file?** The planned failed-words feature will drop known non-words on the phone before uploading a player's failed tries, so the app needs the list. It ships now (about 34 KB) at a path named by `rejectedWordsAsset` in lib/logic/word_list.dart. It's not part of the game dictionary and isn't loaded yet. *Why:* shipping it now means the feature can use it straight away. Keeping it out of `WordListPart` means it can never make a word valid by mistake.
70. **How is it kept in sync with the dictionary?** A test checks that no rejected word is in any dictionary file, in the built dictionary or on the blocklist, that there are no repeats, and that dictionary words plus rejected words cover all 17,576 combinations. `tool/dict/check_duplicates.py` also checks the overlap. If a word is added to the dictionary later, it has to come off the rejected list in the same change, or the tests fail. *Why:* a word can't be both valid and rejected.
71. **What about nig, jap and fgt?** They're on the rejected list because you said "all the others" go there. They're not added to the blocklist, since you haven't decided on that. *Why:* the blocklist is your call. Rejected words are already never accepted.
